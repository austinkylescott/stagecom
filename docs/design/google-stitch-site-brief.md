# Google Stitch Site Brief: Stagecom

## Purpose

Use this brief to generate the design direction for the **entire Stagecom site**, covering the product story and the features currently in scope. The design should communicate that Stagecom is not a generic events tool or a broad creator platform. It is purpose-built for the real operating model of improv communities and can also extend naturally to other performance communities: contextual roles, explicit casting, show ownership, theater oversight, lineup clarity, and reduced off-platform coordination.

The site should feel community-centered first, while still being credible to theater managers, producers, and performers as a serious operating system for local performance communities. It should reflect the real feature set that exists or is actively being built now, while allowing Stitch to propose stronger ways to express that feature set than the current site may use.

Stitch should consider all of the product information in this brief and decide the most effective overall site structure accordingly, rather than being forced into a predetermined page plan.

---

## Product Essence

**Stagecom** is a community management platform for improv theaters, ensembles, and other local performance communities that replaces the messy mix of spreadsheets, email threads, group texts, Discord servers, and social media currently used to run shows and ensembles.

It centralizes:

- performer rosters and contextual roles
- show, practice, workshop, meeting, and audition scheduling
- lineup building and cast management
- invitations and accept/decline flows
- show-day updates and notifications
- theater-level oversight and approvals

Core positioning:

- built for how improv communities actually operate
- private by default, but capable of public show visibility when appropriate
- structured enough for theater operations, but human enough for creative communities
- centered on helping people connect more clearly to their local improv or performance community

Current feature scope to reflect in the site:

- authentication and profiles
- theater creation and membership
- show, practice, meeting, workshop, and audition setup
- contextual roles across theaters and shows
- approval and review workflow
- lineup and program order management
- casting visibility modes
- invitations and accept/decline flows
- in-app and email notifications

Feature emphasis priority:

- highest emphasis: casting flows, lineup clarity, theater management, approvals, and operational coordination
- secondary emphasis: performer discovery of shows and casting opportunities, with auditions as a secondary detail
- supporting emphasis: notifications, profiles, and community participation

## Core Product Truths

These ideas should shape the design and messaging:

1. Roles are contextual, not permanent.
   A performer can also be a producer. A producer is not automatically part of the cast.

2. Cast membership is explicit.
   People are only in a lineup when they are actually added to it.

3. Privacy and trust matter.
   Not everything should be public. The system supports internal coordination and theater-controlled visibility.

4. Producers need operational clarity.
   They need to schedule, cast, communicate, and manage changes without juggling multiple tools.

5. Theater managers need oversight without micromanaging.
   The product should feel organized, reliable, and permission-aware.

6. The product reduces off-platform coordination.
   The goal is fewer side conversations, fewer missed updates, and fewer unclear lineups.

## Visual Direction

Design a bold, community-forward site with a **fun brutalist** visual language. The look should feel culturally specific, memorable, and a little raw in a deliberate way, while still being easy to parse and not intimidating to use.

Visual direction:

- bold black structure using `#2B2926`
- saturated brand accents using `#EAA542`, `#82BFB6`, and `#C76056`
- thick borders and heavy framing
- confident blocks, panels, cards, and section dividers
- graphic, poster-like composition with strong rhythm
- clear hierarchy and highly legible layout despite the bold styling
- community warmth and creative energy over polished corporate slickness

Suggested visual cues:

- subtle stagecraft references, not literal comedy masks
- rehearsal-room energy, local venue flyers, bulletin-board culture, and community arts identity
- layered panels, offset frames, label systems, stamps, tabs, or poster-like callouts
- bold section transitions and strong module separation
- generous contrast and readable spacing so the app still feels approachable

Typography:

- use **Cubano** for logo or high-impact headline moments where appropriate
- pair it with a clean, highly legible supporting typeface for body copy and UI labels
- the typography should feel expressive and cultural, but never sloppy

The result should feel like:
"the shared operating system for a local improv community."

## Site Goals

The generated site should help a visitor understand, within a few seconds:

1. what Stagecom is
2. who it is for
3. that it exists to strengthen connection across a local improv or performance community
4. why it is different from generic scheduling or event tools
5. what problems it removes
6. why a theater, producer, or performer should trust it

The site should also communicate that Stagecom is a deliberate blend of:

- a theater operations tool for admins, producers, and staff
- a community hub that performers actually want to use

## Suggested Information Architecture

### 1. Hero

Lead with community first. Communicate that Stagecom helps improv communities and other local performance scenes stay connected, coordinated, and visible to one another.

Hero should make clear:

- this is for improv theaters and ensembles
- it handles scheduling, casting, lineups, and communication
- it reduces operational chaos
- it helps people feel connected to all parts of their local improv or performance community
- it gives the people running the scene better tools so they can spend less time managing mess

Possible hero framing:

- "A home for your improv community."
- "The operating system for your local scene."
- "Run your theater together."

Supporting copy should reference the fragmented current workflow:
spreadsheets, texts, email threads, Discord, social media, scattered logistics.

Primary CTA ideas:

- Follow the build
- Join the waitlist
- See what Stagecom is building
- Explore the feature set

Secondary CTA ideas:

- For theaters
- For producers
- For performers

Status framing:

- keep the **under construction** message secondary, not central
- make it feel active and in-progress rather than unavailable
- this is not a waitlist-heavy marketing posture; it is a product-and-features posture
- "building with the community" is a better frame than "coming soon"

### 2. Problem Section

Show the current pain clearly:

- missed lineup information
- too many coordination channels
- unclear ownership
- producers doing manual follow-up
- managers lacking visibility

This section should feel specific to theater operations, not generic project management.

### 3. Solution / Product Value

Highlight the current product system:

- theater membership and roles
- show and event creation
- explicit cast management
- invitations and responses
- lineup clarity
- review / approval workflow
- notifications and reminders
- profiles and community participation
- casting visibility modes
- program / rundown organization

This section should give the most visual and narrative weight to:

- casting and invitation flow
- lineup and rundown clarity
- theater-level management and review
- reduced coordination overhead for admins and producers
- performer-facing access to shows and opportunities, with auditions presented as a secondary detail

### 4. Role-Based Value Section

Break the value down by audience:

- for theater managers
- for producers
- for performers

Each role should feel like it has different needs and different reasons to trust the platform.

Important framing:

- admins and producers need one place to manage the moving pieces without living in spreadsheets, email chains, or text threads
- performers need a tool worth returning to because it helps them find shows and casting opportunities inside their community
- auditions can be mentioned, but should stay secondary to the broader story

### 5. Product Screens / UI Showcase

Show realistic product moments such as:

- a show setup screen
- cast invitation and response flow
- lineup / program order view
- theater review queue
- notifications or show-day updates

Use **mock UI placeholders and product concept frames** rather than polished final screenshots. The product UI is still evolving, so the site should imply a strong product direction without pretending the visual system is fully locked.

UI blocks should emphasize:

- clarity
- permissions
- role-aware workflows
- friendliness and low intimidation
- organized information over complex dashboards

Suggested concept modules:

- theater home / membership view
- upcoming shows and practices
- show setup with role and casting controls
- invite / accept / decline flow
- lineup or rundown board
- review queue for theater staff
- notification center or reminder state

### 6. Trust / Principles Section

Use product principles as trust signals:

- privacy by default
- contextual roles
- explicit cast membership
- theater-level oversight
- reduced off-platform communication

### 7. Closing CTA

End with a clear invitation for theaters, producers, or early adopters.

## Functional Content Constraints

Do not imply these are core v1 features:

- built-in ticket sales
- payroll or payouts
- broad marketing automation
- audition marketplace behavior

You may mention future extensibility or public listing readiness, but do not position Stagecom as a ticketing product.

## Design Constraints For Stitch

- Keep the design premium and distinctive, not template-looking.
- Favor strong hierarchy, thick borders, graphic blocks, and confident typography.
- Use visuals that suggest live performance, ensemble coordination, and community infrastructure.
- Do not overuse playful improv tropes.
- Prioritize credibility with theater operators and performers equally.
- Make the product screenshots or UI mock blocks feel clean, legible, role-aware, and welcoming.
- Avoid making the brutalist style feel harsh or difficult to navigate.
- Let the brand colors carry energy; black should provide structure, not heaviness.
- The page should feel culturally alive and community-built, not corporate.
- Match the currently desired feature set, but feel free to express those features more clearly than the current site does.
- Favor feature communication and product clarity over launch-marketing polish.
- Treat casting and theater management as the product center of gravity.
- Present the product as something both operators and performers would actively want to use.
